Geometry is a feature list: shapes, then operations on them, then one overlay that names the regions.

- A 2D model is the x-z cross-section (x lateral, z up). add_rect takes the two corners {min:[x,z], max:[x,z]}; add_circle {center, r}; add_polygon {pts:[[x,z],...]}. list_operations gives every operation with its fields, defaults and a one-line description.
- Features have names (give "name" or accept rect1, rect2). Later features refer to earlier ones by name: add_difference {a:"outer", b:["hole"]}, add_union {ids:[...]}, add_intersect {ids:[...]}.
- add_overlay is the root. regions are in painter order, each {name, input:<feature name>}; where regions overlap the later one wins. So list the big background first (a buried oxide, a cladding) and the core last. Regions named in the overlay are what materials are assigned to.
- Parameters: set_param {name, value} defines a number or an expression other fields can use (width, 2*thickness). update_feature changes fields of a feature; remove_feature, rename_feature, suppress_feature.
- Boundaries get names from the regions they separate ("BOX|Si", the interface), or from a feature edge (box.bottom). geometry_names lists them. A selector is also accepted anywhere a boundary is wanted: "outer & plane(x = 0)".
- Absorbing layers: `add_absorbing_layer` (or `add_pml` with `mode: "Outward"`) extrudes zero-padding Quad/Prism/Hex layers outward across `Cartesian`, `Cylindrical`, or `Spherical` topologies (`thickness` and `layers` default to `"auto"`). Legacy `add_pml` with `window` slices inward. Pieces are named `region.pml_*` and `region*` in a material assignment covers the region and its pieces. Use `add_pml_conditions` (`auto_scale: true` or `strength <= 0` for joint wavelength calibration) and `pml_diagnostics`.
- Mesh size hints: add_size {sel, h} asks for element size h at the selected boundaries or regions. The global size is set_mesh h.
- A 3D model has dim 3 (project_new {dim:3}); a 2D section can also be swept along y with set_mesh {sweep:[{thickness, n}]}, the two ends are then the boundaries ymin and ymax.
- Check before meshing: check_project, geometry_names. Errors name the feature and the reason.

Bodies of revolution (disks, posts, rings): a 2D model whose x is the radius r (the axis is x = 0, a body may touch it), set_study sms axisymmetric_eigenfrequency {n_modes, f_search, azimuthal_order} or ems axisymmetric_modes. For anchor loss add_absorbing_layer or add_pml on the sides XMax and ZMin (never towards the axis), then add_pml_conditions; result scalars fN (Hz), QN and `layer fraction N` (pick modes with a small fraction; modes of the layers have Q near 1), numeric `f re N (Hz)` and `f im N (Hz)`, and the energy shares `radial share N`, `axial share N`, `azimuthal share N`, `share in <region> N` that tell the families apart (m = 0 includes torsional modes, azimuthal share 1). Q converges slower than f; refine at re-entrant corners (add_size on a small region around the corner) and solve again with a smaller size: convergence_check refines the global size and scales add_size and PML layers. Use `match` "nearest" or `track` {f} for complex modes, whose order changes between meshes.
Mesh: nyx.set_mesh(h, adapt_scale, physics_sizing, pml_mode, max_dev, curved). `pml_mode` defaults to `"auto"` (structured Quad/Prism/Hex layers when PML regions exist). In 3D, `curved` true gives cells on curved surfaces and concentric PML shells quadratic geometry.
